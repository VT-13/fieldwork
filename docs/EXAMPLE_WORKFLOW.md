# Example outreach workflow

All example companies and messages in the built-in demo are **fictional**. They illustrate the flow; they are not discovered internship openings.

## First real company

1. **Profile:** Enter your name and email, VEX Robotics World Championship participation, actual website projects, portfolio links, and resume text. Keep skills specific. Do not claim startup or research experience unless you actually have it. Verify the profile.
2. **Discovery:** Search software or robotics near Rocklin at 50 miles. A Google Places result with coordinates gets a calculated distance. Tavily/Apollo candidates stay unverified geographically until you check them.
3. **Research:** Open a promising company and run Research & draft. The worker stores quotes such as a documented product feature or service. No current news, founder biography or internship history is invented to fill gaps.
4. **Contact:** Hunter looks for a suitable public professional contact. If none appears, add a publicly listed founder or engineering contact with the correct name. Run verification; do not override catch-all results.
5. **Draft:** The writer matches one or two company facts to verified student experience. The independent reviewer checks names, evidence, tone, specific personalization and student claims. One regeneration is permitted; further failure goes to review.
6. **Review:** Inspect the evidence and the proposed contribution. Confirm distance and profile details. A company being nearby or student-friendly does not mean it has an available internship.
7. **Send:** Approve the draft. In live mode, the worker checks mailbox identity, recent validation, profile version, quality score, suppression, daily quota and spacing; then syncs the inbox before sending.
8. **Follow up:** At day 7, a new email references the introduction and offers a different concrete idea. Day 14 and day 30 have the same new-value gate. Any reply stops automation; record whether it was positive, an interview, an offer, or a decline.

## Fictional draft

**Subject:** A small dashboard idea for your inspection robots

Hi,

Your focus on inspection robots for small manufacturing teams caught my attention, especially the connection between camera feeds and operator dashboards.

I’m a high school freshman in the Rocklin/Roseville area and a VEX Robotics World Championship competitor. I’ve also built websites for local businesses. I’d be interested in helping prototype a simple dashboard view that makes inspection results easier for an operator to review.

Would you be open to a short conversation about whether a small, supervised project could be useful to your team? I’m happy to start with a clearly scoped task.

If this isn’t a fit, let me know and I won’t follow up.

[Your name]

This is a fixture, not a reusable name-swap template. Live generation must reference the actual company's saved evidence and your verified profile.

## Bounded failure example

A website has no usable product details and the contact verifier reports catch-all. The company is marked for attention. No email is sent, no endless regeneration runs, and the worker proceeds to another company. Useful manual work might be finding one specific product page or a better public contact; an unknown fact is never repaired by guessing.

## Learning example

After enough real outreach, Insights may show that project-match introductions to small robotics firms attracted more replies than other segments. Treat small cohorts as exploratory: exact subject lines are often unique, and an observational difference is not evidence that a phrase caused the reply. Optimize for positive conversations, interviews and offers rather than pixel opens or total send count.
